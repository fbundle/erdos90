# CM-field

## Overview
A CM-field is "a particular type of number field" with "a close connection to the theory of complex multiplication." The abbreviation was "introduced by Shimura and Taniyama."

## Formal Definition
A number field K qualifies as a CM-field if it is "a quadratic extension K/F where the base field F is totally real but K is totally imaginary." This means every embedding of F into ℂ lies within ℝ, but K has no embedding into ℝ.

More specifically, K is generated over F by √α where α is totally negative, ensuring the minimal polynomial of √α over ℚ "has all its roots non-real complex numbers."

## Key Properties
One characteristic is that "complex conjugation on ℂ induces an automorphism on the field which is independent of its embedding into ℂ," changing the sign of √α.

A number field K is a CM-field "if and only if it has a 'units defect'" — containing a proper subfield F whose unit group shares the same ℤ-rank as K.

## Examples
- **Imaginary quadratic fields** (the simplest example)
- **Cyclotomic fields** ℚ(ζₙ), generated by primitive nth roots of unity
- **Abelian varieties**: If V is a complex abelian variety with rank 2n endomorphism algebra, that algebra forms an order in a CM-field
- A **counterexample**: The field defined by x⁴ + x³ − x² − x + 1 is totally imaginary but not CM
