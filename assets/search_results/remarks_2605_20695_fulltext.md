## Overview

This paper presents a human-verified version of an AI-generated counterexample to Erdős's unit distance conjecture, authored by nine prominent mathematicians including Noga Alon, Timothy Gowers, and others.

## Main Result

**Theorem 1.1** establishes that there exists ε > 0 such that point sets in ℝ² can contain at least |𝒫ᵢ|^(1+ε) unit distances, disproving Erdős's conjecture that the maximum is n^(1+o(1)).

## Key Innovation

The proof uses infinite class field towers with bounded root discriminant and takes number field degree to infinity, rather than fixing a field and varying parameters—a paradigm shift from classical approaches.

## Technical Approach

The argument relies on two main lemmas:
- **Lemma 2.1**: Establishes how many magnitude-1 algebraic numbers exist in CM fields with bounded denominators using pigeonhole principles
- **Lemma 2.2**: Constructs lattices in ℂ^f that yield point sets in the plane through geometric projections

The construction employs Golod-Shafarevich towers, concepts from algebraic number theory about class field towers, and splitting behavior of prime ideals.

## Significance

This represents the first major open problem solved by AI without human intervention after training. The reflections from co-authors discuss implications for both mathematics and AI capabilities, noting the proof required "confluence of several unlikely events" that AI's systematic exploration made possible.
