## Context

This paper's techniques are central to the Sawin/OpenAI unit distance construction (arXiv:2605.20579). The approach uses small split primes to bound l-torsion in class groups via a pigeonhole principle on ideal classes — a strategy adapted in the unit distance proof to produce magnitude-1 elements in CM fields rather than class group bounds.

From arXiv:2605.20695 (Alon et al.):
> "The argument relies crucially on ideas that may, at least in retrospect, be attributed to Ellenberg-Venkatesh."

The Ellenberg-Venkatesh technique: using the injection of (Z/lZ)-torsion elements of Cl(K) into unit groups of residue fields at split primes p, one obtains |Cl(K)[l]| ≤ p^[K:Q] for each split prime p ≡ 1 (mod l). Taking the product over many such split primes gives an upper bound on torsion. The dual/reflection version gives a lower bound on the number of norm-1 elements.
